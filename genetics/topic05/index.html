    Dihybrid Cross Ratios from Epistatic Metabolic Pathways (4 Choices)

    MC BBQ Text Blackboard Ultra ZIP Canvas/ADAPT QTI v1.2
    Click to show Dihybrid Cross Ratios from Epistatic Metabolic Pathways (4 Choices) example problem
    Gene 1 Gene 2
    blue ⟶ yellow ⟶ white

    A two step metabolic pathway determines the pigment color. When gene 1 is dominant, the gene expresses an enzyme that converts the blue precursor pigment into the yellow pigment. When gene 1 is homozygous recessive, there is no pigment change and the blue precursor pigment remains. Gene 2 only has an effect on the pigment when gene 1 is dominant. When gene 2 is dominant, the gene expresses an enzyme that converts the yellow pigment into the white pigment.

    The diagram and description above explain the interaction of two genes. Determine the dihybird cross phenotypic ratio.

     

    Test-Cross Ratios from Epistatic F2 Ratios (6 Choices)

    MC BBQ Text Blackboard Ultra ZIP Canvas/ADAPT QTI v1.2
    Click to show Test-Cross Ratios from Epistatic F2 Ratios (6 Choices) example problem

    In a standard dihybrid cross involving two independent genes, the expected F2 phenotypic ratio is 9:3:3:1. A test-cross with an F1 individual and a double recessive usually yields a 1:1:1:1 phenotypic ratio if the genes assort independently.
    In a specific cross, F2 progeny exhibit a modified dihybrid ratio of 10:3:3 (instead of 9:3:3:1).
    What phenotypic ratio would be expected from a test-cross with an individual from the F1 progeny?

    The progeny from the test-cross exhibited a modified ratio of 1:2:1, different from the expected 1:1:1:1 ratio.
    What phenotypic ratio would be expected in the F2 generation if the original dihybrid cross is continued?

     

    Lethal Allele Cross Ratios and Survival Fractions

    MC BBQ Text Blackboard Ultra ZIP Canvas/ADAPT QTI v1.2
    Click to show Lethal Allele Cross Ratios and Survival Fractions example problem

    In cattle, heterozygous offspring (Dd) have dwarf legs. Offspring with two copies of D (DD) do not survive. The table shows what happens with each genotype.

    Genotype key
    Genotype What happens
    DD Does not survive (lethal)
    Dd Survives with dwarf legs
    dd Survives with typical leg length (wildtype)

    A heterozygous parent (Dd) is crossed with a wildtype parent (dd). What fraction of all offspring will not survive?
